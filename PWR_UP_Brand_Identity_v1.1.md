## 0. Executive Summary

PWR UP is a modern active wellness stack brand for people who want simple, science-backed daily support for movement, focus, and recovery.

The brand should not be positioned as a creatine-only brand.

Creatine is the first commercial wedge. The company should be built as a broader daily wellness performance platform from day one.

The core brand system is:

**PWR UP**  
**Perform. Wellness. Recover.**  
**Move. Focus. Recover.**

The first product focus is:

**PWR UP Move**  
Creatine-based morning stack  
For daily strength, movement, and active living.

The brand must feel:

- modern
- active
- warm
- premium but approachable
- science-backed but human
- trusted
- halal-conscious
- BPOM-aware
- made for real Indonesian active life

PWR UP must not feel:

- hardcore gym-bro
- aggressive pre-workout
- childish candy
- overly clinical
- fake biohacker
- cheap marketplace supplement
- overclaiming wellness brand

The strategic truth behind the brand is:

> **The best supplement is the one people can repeat.**

This line should guide product, taste, packaging, content, pricing, and retention decisions.

PWR UP should feel built for Indonesia through halal-conscious sourcing, BPOM pathway, local manufacturing, local taste cues, and practical use cases.

Taste is the reason the habit survives.

If people cannot repeat the product for 28 days, the product is not good enough.

## 21. Main Positioning Risks

### Risk 1: Too much sports-performance language

This creates compliance risk and narrows the audience.

### Risk 2: Too much candy language

This reduces trust and makes dosage or efficacy feel questionable.

### Risk 3: Too much wellness vagueness

This makes the product sound generic and soft.

### Risk 4: Too many ingredients

This makes claims, formulation, BPOM, taste, stability, and consumer understanding harder.

### Risk 5: Too many SKUs too early

This dilutes focus before product-market fit.

### Risk 6: Too premium but not clear

Minimal design without clear benefit can fail in marketplace environments.

### Risk 7: Too founder-centric

Founder story is useful, but the customer must remain the hero.

## 24. Launch Beachhead

For launch, avoid targeting “everyone active.”

Start narrow.

### Primary Launch Beachhead

Urban active professionals who train 2 to 4 times per week and want a simpler supplement habit.

### Secondary Launch Beachhead

Women entering strength, running, padel, Pilates, or hybrid fitness who are curious about creatine but afraid of bulky or gym-bro positioning.

### Tertiary Launch Beachhead

Recreational athletes in running, padel, gym, boxing, cycling, and boutique fitness communities.

### Why This Matters

If PWR UP only wins male gym users who already understand creatine, it risks becoming a narrow sports supplement brand.

If PWR UP also wins women, active parents, and wellness-curious professionals, it becomes a broader active wellness brand.

## 27. Barriers To Purchase

Common barriers:

- creatine confusion
- fear of bloating
- fear of becoming bulky
- concern about sugar
- concern about fake dosage
- concern that gummies are just candy
- price compared with powder
- skepticism toward new local brands
- halal and BPOM uncertainty
- “will this actually work?” doubt
- “will I remember to take this daily?” doubt

### How To Overcome These Barriers

Creatine confusion:  
Use simple education.

Bloating:  
Do not overpromise. Explain serving and usage clearly. Encourage taking with food and water as appropriate.

Bulky fear:  
Position as daily strength and active living, not bodybuilding.

Sugar concern:  
Be transparent about sugar profile.

Candy concern:  
Show clear daily serving, ingredient transparency, quality testing, BPOM pathway, and halal readiness.

Price concern:  
Compare to convenience and routine adherence, not just ingredient cost.

Trust concern:  
Show PWR UP Standard, founder story, clear formulation, and quality process.

## 48. Sensory Identity

PWR UP is an edible brand.

The identity is not only visual. It is also taste, texture, smell, color, and repeatability.

### Sensory Standard: Gummy

Texture: adult chew, not kids candy jelly.  
Mouthfeel: not sandy, not gritty.  
Sweetness: daily-friendly, not too sweet.  
Acidity: fresh but not harsh.  
Coating: premium, not sticky.  
Color: honey-gold or pale yellow, slightly opaque.  
Smell: light honey-citrus, not medicinal.  
Aftertaste: clean finish, no harsh artificial sweetener tail.  
Repeat test: would the customer take this for 28 days?

### Sensory Standard: Drink Mix

Dissolution: easy to dissolve in cold water.  
Mouthfeel: not chalky, not sandy.  
Taste: refreshing, not medicinal.  
Sweetness: low sugar / no sugar preferred, but taste must still work.  
Acidity: fresh but not too sharp.  
Mineral taste: controlled, not salty or metallic.  
Color: light, refreshing, not artificial-looking.  
Aftertaste: clean finish.  
Repeat test: would the customer drink this 3 to 5 times per week or daily?

### Sensory Rule

If product experience does not support repeat behavior, the brand promise fails.
